    How the rating works

    home margin = rhome − raway + H + B × (away on back-to-back − home on back-to-back) + noise

    • Every game in the league is used, so each opponent is itself adjusted for who it played. The ratings are solved together so the predicted margins fit the actual ones as closely as possible.
    • Home court, H = 2.0 points. Recent estimates are 1.8 (2020–26 seasons, Sparkle Technologies) and 2.08 (2022–25, VSiN).
    • Rest, B = 1.0 point against a team playing the second night of a back-to-back. Season-by-season estimates run from about 0.5 to 2 points (The Data Jocks).
    • Small-sample shrinkage adds the equivalent of 5 games of average play to every team, so a team's rating moves toward its results as it plays more. The 5 comes from game-to-game noise of about 12 points (Wayne Winston) and last season's spread of team strength, about 5.2–5.8 points (StatMuse 2025-26 net ratings). Turn it off to see results-only ratings, which swing wildly this early in the season.
    • Ratings average zero across the league. Close games are decided by 5 points or fewer, or in overtime.
    • ± is one standard error, from game-to-game noise of 12 points and the shrinkage above. About two times in three, a team's true strength sits inside rating ± that number. With shrinkage off it is approximated as 12 ÷ √games.
    • Per 100 possessions. Possessions ≈ FGA + 0.44 × FTA − offensive rebounds + turnovers, averaged over the team and its opponents. Pace is possessions per 48 minutes.
    • Scorecard. For each game date, the ratings are refit using only earlier games, then each game that day is projected. Nothing from the game itself or later games leaks in.
    • With known absences (Proposed method). A missing player's value is (his box plus-minus + 2) × his minutes per game ÷ 48, where +2 measures him against a replacement-level player. His BPM blends this season with last season, last season counting as 20 games. That value is scaled by the share of games he has played, because a team that has already played without him is already rated without him. Only absences on file are used; NBA.com inactive lists describe games already played, so they never change a projection.

    Not built in, shown as context: the four factors, three-point luck, player stats and quarter splits. Absences adjust the projections only, shown next to the healthy projection, never the ratings themselves.

    Focus team. Pick any team at the top and every section follows it: the title, the next game and spotlight, players, quarters and game-by-game four factors. The highlight color is the focus team's own color (teamcolorcodes.com; Clippers and Jazz from Design Your Way, the Jazz value approximate), lightened or darkened where needed so text on it stays readable.

    Updating. Scores, box scores, player lines, quarter scores and inactive lists for all 30 teams come from NBA.com through a script run on a PC (tools/nba in the repository); hand-checked files cover the Lakers until that data is on file, and NBA.com takes precedence wherever both exist. Season totals are summed from the game box scores once every game is on file. Everything on this page recalculates from those files.

    Raw margins are each team's average point differential with no adjustment at all. Projections and the game log always use the adjusted rating.
